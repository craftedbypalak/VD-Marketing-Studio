## 2. The shape of the year

**Diwali 2026 to Holi 2027** stands as already planned: the dark block 7 to 11 November, Chhath on Sunday 15 November, Guru Nanak Jayanti on Tuesday 24 November, the first wedding cluster 21 November to 12 December, Kharmas from Dhanu Sankranti on 16 December to Makar Sankranti on 15 January (no weddings; the best owner-attention window), the Budget on Monday 1 February (expected), Ramadan from about 8 or 9 February, Maha Shivaratri on Saturday 6 March, Eid al-Fitr on 10 March (gazetted; 9 March on the tentative MP list), Holi on 21 to 22 March with Rangpanchami and the Indore Ger on Saturday 27 March, and the financial year-end on 31 March.

**April to July 2027** is the second wedding season. Kharmas runs 15 March to 14 April (Meena to Mesha Sankranti), so Chaitra Navratri and Gudi Padwa (7 April), Ambedkar Jayanti (14 April), Ram Navami (15 April) and Mahavir Jayanti (19 April) fall in a wedding-free stretch. Muhurats then run thick: 9 in April from the 18th, 18 in May, 16 in June and 6 in July, ending 12 July. Akshaya Tritiya is Sunday 9 May, Eid al-Adha Monday 17 May (gazetted; 16 May on most Islamic calendars), Buddha Purnima Thursday 20 May, and Ashura 15 or 16 June. Devshayani Ekadashi on Wednesday 14 July opens Chaturmas and closes weddings until Devutthana Ekadashi on 10 November.

**August to mid-October** is festival-dense but wedding-free: Independence Day and Milad-un-Nabi share Sunday 15 August (Drik Panchang gives Milad on the 16th), Raksha Bandhan Tuesday 17 August, Janmashtami Wednesday 25 August, Paryushan 27 August to Samvatsari 3 September (expected), Ganesh Chaturthi Saturday 4 September, Indore's Anant Chaturdashi night procession Tuesday 14 September, Pitru Paksha 16 to 29 September, Navratri and the Indore garba season 30 September to 8 October, and Dussehra Saturday 9 October.

**Diwali 2027** falls on Friday 29 October, with Dhanteras Wednesday 27 October, Naraka Chaturdashi Thursday 28, Govardhan Puja Saturday 30 and Bhai Dooj Sunday 31 October, which is also the ITR date for audit cases. Treat 28 October to 31 October as dark. Chhath is Thursday 4 November, the wedding season reopens 10 November with 11 November muhurats, Guru Nanak Jayanti is Sunday 14 November, seven December muhurats end on 14 December, Kharmas begins 16 December, and Christmas falls on Saturday 25 December.

**Compliance rhythm.** Every month: TDS on the 7th, GSTR-1 on the 11th, GSTR-3B on the 20th; quarterly QRMP filers on the 13th and the 22nd (Madhya Pradesh is in the 22nd-day group). Whole days lost: advance tax on 15 December, 15 March, 15 June and 15 September; TDS statements on 31 January, 31 May, 31 July and 31 October; ITR for non-audit taxpayers on Saturday 31 July; tax audit reports on 30 September; audit-case ITRs on 31 October; GSTR-9/9C and belated returns on 31 December. The JSON marks weekday compliance days **quiet** and weekend or whole-day deadlines **dark**, consistent with the existing calendar.

## 3. Which festivals a business contact expects a greeting for

No survey measures what Indian B2B contacts expect; what exists is trade commentary. The consistent findings are that generic greetings add nothing, that a short typed note from the account owner outperforms a designed card, and that a greeting with a sales line attached reads as advertising ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2017/10/brands-festive-marketing); [Agency Reporter](https://agencyreporter.com/?p=84138); [RichAutomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/diwali-wishes-message-for-customers-whatsapp-templates-india-2026); [Zenweb](https://zenweb.my/blog/festive-b2b-marketing)). A gifting vendor's claim that skipping Diwali "can be interpreted as indifference" is sales copy, but it matches Indore practice ([Chococraft](https://www.chococraft.in/blogs/corporate-diwali-gifts/roi-risks-skipping-diwali-gifts)).

**Expected (everyone):** Diwali, New Year, Holi, Dussehra, Republic Day, Independence Day, Makar Sankranti (strongly in Indore), Gudi Padwa / Hindu New Year for Marathi and Sindhi contacts. **Expected by community, with customary wording:** Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha ("Eid Mubarak"), Ramadan's first day ("Ramadan Mubarak"), Guru Nanak Jayanti ("Gurpurab di lakh lakh vadhaiyan"), Christmas ("Merry Christmas"), Mahavir Jayanti and Samvatsari ("Michhami Dukkadam", sent as plain text to Jain contacts, a large part of Indore's trading community), Akshaya Tritiya, Chhath for Bihar and eastern UP contacts, Vishwakarma Puja for factory owners. **Welcome but optional:** Dhanteras, Maha Shivaratri, Ram Navami, Janmashtami, Ganesh Chaturthi, Navratri, Vasant Panchami, Buddha Purnima, Ambedkar Jayanti, MP Foundation Day, Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Jayanti (Indore). **Noise, never greet from a firm:** Bhai Dooj, Govardhan Puja, Karva Chauth, Raksha Bandhan (a family day; at most a status), Guru Purnima, Tulsi Vivah, Sharad Purnima, Hanuman Jayanti and the jayantis on the MP list (Ravidas, Parashurama, Valmiki, Birsa Munda). **Never greet:** Good Friday (send Easter instead), Muharram and Ashura, Guru Teg Bahadur Martyrdom Day, Pitru Paksha, and any mourning day.

## 4. Greeting etiquette

- **Eve or day.** Diwali, Christmas and New Year: the evening before, or the morning of the day, before 10 am. Eid: the evening the moon is sighted (Chand Raat) or Eid morning, never the day after. Holi: Holika Dahan evening or early Dhulendi morning. Makar Sankranti in Indore: the evening of 14 January covers both reckonings. Gurpurab, Mahavir Jayanti, Samvatsari, Dussehra, Republic and Independence Day: on the day, by 9 am.
- **Plain text, not a designed card.** A typed message under 30 words with the contact's name and a "ji" suffix, signed "Palak Gupta, Venture Drafters", on WhatsApp one-to-one or email for formal relationships; the firm's designed card goes only on Instagram and WhatsApp status, once. Mass forwards and broadcast lists are counterproductive and WhatsApp Business policy requires opt-in for templates.
- **No call to action** within two days either side of Diwali, Holi, Eid and Dussehra; resume with back-to-work utility on the first full working day.
- **Mourning rule** (unchanged): pause every channel within the hour on the death of a national figure or a mass-casualty event, dark through the funeral, non-promotional posts only from day three of declared mourning; plain-text condolence optional, never a creative.

## 5. Corrections to the existing list

1. **Govardhan Puja 2026** falls on Tuesday 10 November by tithi (Pratipada 12:31 pm 9 Nov to 2:00 pm 10 Nov, [Drik Panchang](https://www.drikpanchang.com/festivals/govardhan-puja/govardhan-puja-date-time.html?year=2026)); the MP list observes the holiday on Monday 9 November. Both days are already dark.
2. **Eid al-Fitr 2027** is 10 March on the central gazetted list and Drik Panchang; the existing 9 March is the Saudi-calendar date. Keep both days greeting-only.
3. **Indore Marathon**: the official site names no 2027 date; AIMS lists 20 December 2026 and 14 March 2027, aggregators mid-February. All three dates are recorded as unconfirmed.
4. **Mahavir Jayanti** is Monday 19 April (gazetted and Drik Panchang), not the 18 April of the tentative MP list; **Guru Nanak Jayanti 2027** is Sunday 14 November, not 13 November.
5. **Holi** is 22 March in Indore; the central list observes 23 March. Both are recorded.
6. Bhai Dooj and Chhath appear as regional holidays on third-party MP lists; the official notification has not been verified.
