# Founder LinkedIn Content in 2026: Research Note for Venture Drafters

Research date: 29 October 2026. Labels: **[E]** means a published source states it (URL and date given); **[I]** means my inference. This note builds on `Marketing/_research/content-strategy-2026-10/research-linkedin-instagram.md` (23 October 2026), which already covers profile-versus-page reach, format multipliers, the link penalty, posting frequency and newsletters. Those findings are not repeated.

Caution: nearly every figure below comes from a vendor selling LinkedIn tooling. LinkedIn itself published only two relevant primary documents in 2026: the feed-engineering post of 12 March and the help page defining Saves and Sends. Methodologies are rarely disclosed, figures conflict, and datasets skew Western. Venture Drafters' own analytics after twelve weeks should override anything here.

---

## 1. Which content kinds earn reach and conversations

**[E] Mylance, "LinkedIn Engagement Statistics (2026)", 895 posts, 37 creators, B2B-leaning.** Each post was scored against its author's own median engagement; "beat rate" is the share at or above that median. By archetype: Journey/Milestone 72%, Story 60%, Confession 59%, Contrarian 57%, Insight 55% (n=432), Promotional 52%. High-specificity posts beat baseline 68% of the time against about 50% for vague posts; a number in the first line, 61% versus 52%; image versus none, 61% versus 54%. Dated only "2026". https://www.mylance.co/linkedin-engagement-statistics

**[E] Usera, Cox and Walker, SAGE Open, 15 March 2026, 1,001 users' posts.** Business posts were most frequent, but Interpersonal (praise of others) and Observance posts drew more reactions and comments; Business and Expertise posts were reposted more. Posts about one's own achievements earned more comments but not more reactions. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440261428891

**[E] Van der Blom, Creator Science podcast, 2 June 2026 (1.3 million posts, about 50,000 creators).** Reach down about 60% over two years for active creators; engagement down only 20 to 25%. He prefers text-plus-image and carousels; text-only "wins" only for the top 5% of creators; standalone articles run at about 0.4x; newsletter editions outperform posts and articles. No story-versus-teaching breakdown. https://creatorscience.com/podcast/richard-van-der-blom-2/

**No evidence found** for a controlled comparison of story, teaching and achievement posts on founder accounts. Mix ratios in circulation ("50% educational, 40% personal, 10% broad") are practitioner opinion.

**[I]** The signal is not "story beats teaching" but specificity and personal stake. Journey posts win because they are concrete and dated; Insight posts lag because most are generic. The SAGE finding that praise of others earns the most reactions matters in Indore's reference-driven market: a post crediting a client's owner, plant head or CA by name (with permission) is both the most engaging type and the most credible. Achievement posts earn comments and belong in the mix, at no more than one in five. Recommended mix: 40% field observations, 30% teaching assets, 20% journey or milestone, 10% praise of a client or peer.

## 2. Format, length, hook

**[E] Dataslayer, 12 February 2026 (updated July 2026), citing van der Blom and Sprout Social:** average engagement, document 6.60%, native video (30 to 90 s) 5.60%, image-plus-text 3.20%, text-only 2.00%; polls at 0.07% after a March 2026 change. Recommends 5 to 10 slides at 1080x1350, engagement dropping after slide 10 (unsourced). https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now

**[E] Video.** A paired test of 184 posts found video impressions at 3.4x the text version but comments at 0.7x; 35 to 60 seconds performed best (Meet Lea, 2026). Hootsuite (14 July 2026) relays LinkedIn's ad guidance: under 30 seconds for awareness, 30 to 90 for upper funnel. The "7-second hold rate" claim is unverified. https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-video-performance ; https://blog.hootsuite.com/ideal-social-media-post-length/

**[E] Text length.** Mylance: under 400 characters beats median only 33% of the time; 400 and above, 53 to 59%; over 2,000, 59%. Vendor recommendations cluster at 1,300 to 1,900 characters.

**[E] The "see more" cut.** Sources conflict: about 210 characters desktop and 140 mobile (Supergrow, LinkedInPreview, 2026); Hootsuite gives about 140. No LinkedIn confirmation. **[I]** Write the hook to survive 140 characters; 210 is the desktop ceiling, not the target.

**[I] When to use each.** Text plus one real photo as the default. PDF carousel for anything a reader would save (checklists, before-and-after process maps, SOP excerpts), 6 to 10 slides. Native vertical video only for a walk-through or a client's floor, 35 to 60 seconds, captions burned in. Text-only never.

## 3. Days, times (IST), and the first hour

**[E] MagicPost, updated 2 May 2026, 220,220 Indian posts, each scored against its author's own average.** Best hour 8 am IST (81/100), 9 am close behind. Top slots: Monday 9 am (100), Monday 8 am (96), Wednesday 8 am (96), Friday 8 am (88). Evenings plateau at 62 to 63; weekends lower. The only India dataset found with a stated sample. https://magicpost.in/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin-india

**[E] Buffer, 9 September 2026, 4.8 million posts, reader-local time.** Best day Wednesday, then Thursday, Friday; Monday worst; peak 3 to 8 pm; no India breakdown. https://www.buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin

**[E]** Other India vendors (Reactin, RecurPost, Postpone, WSCube, 2026) give Tuesday to Thursday, 9 am to 12 pm IST, plus 6 to 8 pm for B2B; methods undisclosed.

**[I]** Buffer's afternoon peak reflects a global, reader-local sample; MagicPost measures Indian authors against themselves and is the better guide here: weekday 8 to 9 am IST, Monday and Wednesday preferred, Tuesday and Thursday as fallback. Test 6 to 7 pm once.

**First hour. [E]** Van der Blom posts only when he can reply for 10 to 15 minutes immediately; 2026 is "nurturing posts, not just publishing them". **[E]** Dataslayer: only 5% of posts weak in the first hour recover (unsourced). **[I]** Never schedule into a slot where the founder cannot reply for the next 45 to 60 minutes.

## 4. The company page

**[E]** Page organic reach about 1.6% of followers in 2025, down from 7% in 2021 (DSMN8, 2026). https://dsmn8.com/blog/linkedin-organic-reach-investigation/

**[E]** A direct repost is a "lower-weight share signal"; a repost with added thoughts is "treated closer to original content". Several accounts resharing inside a narrow window may be read as a suppression signal; stagger timing (Ordinal, 2026). https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/how-to-repost-on-linkedin

**No evidence found** that a page reshare reduces the original post's reach; the only risk named is simultaneous coordinated resharing.

**[E]** September 2026: Company Page verification (Premium) and a cleanup tool for false employee listings. https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/new-linkedin-features-and-updates

**[I]** No original page posts. Reshare one founder post every one to two weeks with a one-line comment, at least two hours after the original. Verify the page when offered. The page exists for the logo on the Experience entry and to make Thought Leader Ads possible later.

## 5. Comments and DMs

**[E]** Van der Blom: comments now shape LinkedIn's interest graph; in-theme comments outside your network help, off-topic ones dilute how LinkedIn categorises you; he comments 25 to 30 minutes daily; pods are detected with about 97% accuracy. **[E]** Since 9 August 2026 comments are ordered by relevance to each viewer, so being first no longer guarantees position (Social Media Today via Relevant Audience, 10 August 2026). https://www.relevantaudience.com/social-media-marketing/linkedin-comment-ranking-relevance-update/ Vendor claims such as "15 comments a day yields 100 profile visits" are unverified.

**[E] DM benchmarks (global, vendor, 2026):** cold DMs 8 to 12% reply; personalised no-pitch openers 15 to 40%; connection acceptance 15 to 20% cold versus 55 to 70% when the prospect has seen your content; a pitch in the connection note cuts acceptance 40 to 60%; messages under 80 words reply at twice the rate of those over 150 (Skylead, Reactin, Monolit). **No India-specific DM evidence found**; the one India-facing piece (LexiConn, 15 May 2026) is opinion. https://www.lexiconn.in/knowledge/blogs/linkedin-high-intent-signals-india.html **[E]** A vendor survey of 500+ Indian SMBs (HelloGrowth, January to April 2026) reports WhatsApp as the first sales-contact channel for about two-thirds. https://hellogrowthcrm.com/resources/original-research

**[I]** Comment only within the theme (Indian SME operations, finance, succession), on posts by MP owners, CAs, successors, industry bodies and lenders: 15 to 20 minutes daily, 5 to 8 comments of two or more sentences that add a fact or question. Connection notes: one line referencing their post, no pitch. First DM only after a reaction, comment or save; under 80 words; one question; offer WhatsApp. Measure replies and WhatsApp handoffs, not comment position.

## 6. New in 2026

- **[E] Feed rebuilt, 12 March 2026.** LinkedIn Engineering (Hristo Danchev) replaced multi-source retrieval with one LLM-embedding pipeline and a transformer "Generative Recommender" modelling each member's last 1,000+ interactions. Content is retrieved by semantic similarity, so posts compete on topic, not connection. https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed **[I]** Headline, About and every post should share vocabulary.
- **[E] Saves and Sends** are official metrics (LinkedIn Help, https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a701208 ; profile analytics and API from 21 April 2026).
- **[E] Comments ranked per viewer**, 9 August 2026; an estimated 30% of comments in April to June 2026 were wholly AI-generated (Pangram Labs, via the same source).
- **[E] Video:** vertical feed, playback speed, CapCut publishing; "5x reach" claims unsupported.
- **[E] Newsletters:** open to all, video covers, email metrics; van der Blom attributes about 80% of his conversions to them. Part 1's advice to hold still applies.
- **[E] Thought Leader Ads:** eligible on single image, video, article/newsletter and event posts; not on documents, polls or multi-image; author approval required; 2.68% CTR and $2.29 CPC (ZenABM, 119 ads, 2026); LinkedIn claims 252% higher CTR and 62% lower CPC than single-image ads (Factors.ai, 25 August 2026). https://www.factors.ai/blog/linkedin-thought-leader-ads **[I]** Use only after organic posts prove themselves, for a small MP-targeted push.
- **[E] Other:** "AI slop" report option and AI labels (30 July 2026); Post Proofreader (August 2026); colleague vouching (September 2026). https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/new-linkedin-features-and-updates
