The storyline

The tools are how we run it.

Decided 29 October 2026. Venture Drafters builds the tools that run a business, on the owner's own Google account, and runs them with the owner every week. Nothing in the existing material was ever about startup funding; what changes is the frame of the solution.

Stays

  • Run the business. Not the follow-ups. The promise.
  • See everything. Chase nothing. The tagline.
  • It is a design problem, not a discipline problem. The idea under the house: work has no home, no visible state, no feedback loop.
  • We run it with you, every week. A named person, a Monday hand-out, a Friday score.
  • We run the system. Your team still does the work. The honest limit, beside every promise.
  • Built for my family's firm in Indore first, and used there every day.

Changes

  • Solutions are named as tools we built and run: the reminder desk, the question tray, the weekly score, My Day, the step-by-step job flow. Never a vague "system", never a tool that works without people.
  • "This is not new software" is no longer the opener. It stays as the answer to one fear: "we tried software and it died."
  • The case studies show a tool being designed and what it changed, one VSP system a month.
  • The brief (00-brief.md) sections 1 and 4 are edited to this frame, and that edit is the first item in the review queue.
Every piece, in every channel, is written in one order: Problem, Impact, Solution. Nothing opens on us. The owner must think "that is my day" before we say a word about tools.

The mix

LinkedIn, per 10 posts

  • 4 field observations: one concrete scene from an Indian owner-led business, the diagnosis, no pitch.
  • 3 teaching pieces: a checklist, a rule, a before-and-after system map, usually the fortnightly PDF carousel.
  • 2 journey or milestone posts: what she built, what changed at VSP, one honest limit. Milestone posts beat their median most often in the only archetype study found.
  • 1 post praising a client, peer or host: praise of others draws the most comments in the one peer-reviewed study found.

Every post names something concrete: a business type, a city, a process, a number. The checker flags a generic draft.

Instagram, by pillar (the brief)

  • About half: the problem from the inside. The 7 pm, the phone, the "ho gaya?"
  • About a third: the science. Work needs a home, a name, a date, a next step.
  • About a sixth: a glimpse of a real tool, masked, never the tour.
  • Across all three: built to be sent. A checklist, a rule, a before-and-after an owner forwards to his son or his CA.

Reels for reach, carousels for saves (nine times the saves of a single image in the largest 2026 dataset). The same carousel resized to 4:5 serves Instagram two days after LinkedIn.

The calendar

A slot table, not a blank month.

The calendar is generated from the accounts' weekly and monthly rules and the India business calendar. Every slot knows its account, time and kind. Creating a piece from a slot gives it that date. Moving a piece is a drag on the Mac or a date picker on the phone, and the tool refuses a dark day or a slot of the wrong kind with the reason.

Allotted slots

Monday Reel 7:30 pm. Tuesday LinkedIn 8:30 am and Status 9 am. Wednesday Trial Reel (optional) and, in alternate weeks, the PDF carousel at 4:30 pm. Thursday LinkedIn (weeks without a carousel), carousel 7:30 pm, Status 9 am. Friday page reshare 11 am. Saturday Status optional. Monthly: the second Wednesday note and the second Saturday family Status. The standing rules

Queue nothing more than 48 hours ahead. Post only when Palak can reply for the following hour. The mourning rule: a "go dark" switch in settings marks every slot dark from today until it is turned off. No topical tie-ins on news days. Greetings on the eve, as plain text.

What the checker enforces

  • Course terms and internal system names: blocked anywhere.
  • "Runs without you", "autopilot", "assigns itself": blocked as a hook or headline.
  • Emoji, exclamation marks, hashtags in a LinkedIn body, a sixth Instagram hashtag, text over the platform limit: blocked.
  • A rupee, hours or percentage figure not marked as an example; a firm-like name not on the allowed list; a whole Hindi sentence in our voice; a solution line that names no real tool: warned.
  • Every violation cites the brief line it enforces.

How the AI is kept in register

  • It reads the brief's rules as data: the say list, the never list, the claims allowed and blocked, the storyline frame.
  • It may name only the tools in the public-safe Solutions list. No sales document, no internal name, no client reaches it.
  • Its examples are signed-off copy: the headline bank and elevator pitches, then approved pieces once four exist of a type.
  • The register paragraph in every prompt: a professional firm addressing a managing director; short sentences; warm, never casual; numbers only from the owner or as a question.

The review of every existing piece

On import, every one of the 23 animated Reels, 10 episodes, 9 carousels, 12 b-roll scripts, 18 LinkedIn posts, the Status rules and greetings enters as version 1, exactly as it stands. A review pass then proposes version 2 for each with a change note: the register, the Problem-Impact-Solution order (the middle beat is an impact in every case), the tools frame in the solution lines, the retired headlines removed. Palak sees original and proposal side by side and decides. Nothing is overwritten.

YouTube

One episode a fortnight, made the way the VSP film was made.

Decided 30 October 2026: one long video every other Monday at 11:00 from 16 November 2026, with Shorts between so the channel never has fewer than two uploads a month. The supply is the series Built to Run: Stories, ten 30-minute episodes written and fact-checked on 29 and 30 October (Marketing/case-studies/): the dabbawalas, Asian Paints, Maggi, Amul, Aravind, DMart and Subhiksha, IndiGo, Toyota, McDonald's and Zara, each with its script, b-roll list, slide outline, fact sheet and cut-downs, placed in Studio on the ten fortnightly Mondays from 16 November to 22 March. The VSP film follows on the next fortnight, 5 April. The VSP-system case studies below join the alternate Mondays once one is produced. Each case study is one VSP system: how it was designed and what it changed. The subject order starts with the reminder desk, because follow-up is the problem owners name most. The production follows the pipeline that made the VSP promotional film: plan the script, plan the picture, pull the b-roll, cut it to the voice, then blend in Palak's own clips at a few moments. Most of the picture is b-roll and the screen; her face appears three times.

OutlinePick the system, add notes. The tool drafts six chapters with minutes: the owner's problem as a scene (3 to 5), what it cost VSP in VSP's own words (3 to 5), the design question (5), the build on the masked copy (15 to 25), what changed and the honest limit (5), how an owner starts with one process (3). Each chapter lists what is on screen, the questions to answer to camera, and Shorts candidates.
ScriptWritten chapter by chapter in spoken register, about 140 words a minute, numbers and acronyms marked as they should be read. The problem and its cost land inside the first 30 seconds; no channel intro, no branding first.
ScenesOne row per scene: what is said, what is seen, the kind (b-roll, screen, face, card), stock search words, seconds. Face scenes are limited to the problem, the "what changed" and the close.
FootageStock clips searched and fetched through the same runner the film used; a footage list and a shot plan say what to film at VSP (the masked copy, the coordinator's screen, the cabin door). Every screen frame is checked for real names at full size.
VoiceThe paste-ready script for Palak's own recording (or the voice service the pipeline supports). The picture is cut to the returned voice automatically, word by word.
Own clipsPalak records her three to-camera moments on her phone and uploads them. Each clip is mapped to its scene and replaces the placeholder.
Edit and verifyThe hand-off writes the project folder with the script, brand file, scene plan and the exact commands; Claude Code runs the brand-video pipeline: music and sound under the voice, a frame-exact 1080p MP4, a verification that the words on screen match the script.
Publish setTitle under 70 characters, task first. Description with the outcome in the first 150 characters, chapter timestamps, website link. Three thumbnail variants. End screen with the next case study. Pinned comment with one next step. Two to four Shorts of 20 to 45 seconds, re-hooked, Related video set, posted 6 to 9 pm over the following fortnight. Auto-captions corrected by hand.
AI and cost. The scripts, scene plans and Shorts cuts are drafted through Claude Code on the subscription, not through a billed API key. Only a voice service, if used instead of Palak's own voice, costs money. No AI label is needed for scripts, captions or her own voice; one would be needed only for a realistic synthetic scene, which the plan never uses.

Stories

For existing followers; resharing a feed post to Stories counts for nothing in reach. Repurposing

Each case study yields 4 to 6 vertical clips; the strongest 2 to 4 become Reels and Shorts, each re-hooked on its strongest line, 30 to 60 seconds, captions inside the safe zone (269 px top, 672 px bottom, 65 px sides). Repurposed clips stay under half of all Reels; the rest are native. Own clips count as original; identical re-uploads as Trial Reels are downranked, so trials carry a new hook or style only.
Palak's account publishes the Monday Reel and invites the company page as collaborator. The company page publishes the Thursday carousel and shares it to Palak's Stories. The same file is never posted twice.

Network

A separate tool, for the founder only.

The network brings the calls, and it is not marketing. On 29 October 2026 Palak decided that the people, their tiers, their consent and every touch live in their own tool, Network (Venture Drafters/Network/, port 4748), which only the founder opens. Marketing staff use Studio and never see a name or a number.

The calendar's definition of each festival: the date, the posting rule, the community it is for and the tiers it goes to (Diwali to all four, Holi to Platinum and Gold, Eid to three tiers of the Muslim community). A definition, never a list of people.

Two read-only routes behind a shared token: the events in a date range, with the one-to-one wording, and the published pieces, as something worth sending. Nothing flows the other way; Studio never calls Network.

The tool

Studio: plain JavaScript, your data, AI on the subscription.

Built in a new folder beside the marketing repository. Tonight it runs on the Mac. The same front end can be published on the website later with a hosted database behind it. Every design choice below is written in docs/ARCHITECTURE.md and docs/MODULES.md so anyone can build on it.

Why plain JavaScript

The website is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step; the tool matches it, so one person can maintain both. No framework, no bundler, no TypeScript. The server is Node with no dependencies.

Why Claude Code for the AI

Every AI operation runs claude -p with a fixed system prompt and a JSON schema, on the subscription already paid for. The same function can call the Claude API with a key when the tool is hosted. The brief, the solutions list and the examples travel with every call.

Why a hosted database later, not tonight

A static page on the website cannot hold keys or post anywhere. Supabase gives a database, sign-in, file storage and a function that holds the keys. The store interface is written so the swap is one adapter, and the plan says so in writing before a line of hosted code exists.

What posting will look like, when it comes

Direct where a platform allows it from a self-serve API (Facebook page, Instagram, YouTube, Palak's personal LinkedIn), a one-tap hand-off where it does not (the company LinkedIn page without partner approval, WhatsApp Status, every one-to-one message), or a scheduling service for the rest. Phase 1 leaves every approved piece with its renditions and pictures ready for any of those routes.

Measure

Replies and calls, not likes.

Per post

Real replies (a question or "that is my day"), messages started, calls booked, sends and shares, saves. On LinkedIn: comments from target profiles, profile views, inbound DMs, hand-offs to WhatsApp. On YouTube: retention and External traffic.

Per person

Touches, replies, meetings, referrals given and received, "watched a video before the call". The persona (founder, or son or daughter) and the source of every reply.

The Friday review, 15 minutes

Posted on plan? Which post earned replies; read every one and copy the owner's exact words into the quote bank. Which replies became messages and calls. Which post was sent most. Who went silent. After eight weeks, compare the slots and the accounts, and change the data, not the plan.
Targets to 28 February 2027. Three founding clients signed; two is the floor. Twenty-five real replies in November across both accounts. Every prospect has watched one video before the first call. Follower counts are ignored for the first six months.
