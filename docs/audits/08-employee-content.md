# Audit 8: the marketing employee, on the content

Date: 30 October 2026, on the tool after the marketing director's rework (commit 9ede8cc), the runner queue answering every AI step by hand. Built-in browser, desktop and 375 px, working as the example staff user (non-approver), then as Palak for the approval. Walked the employee's day: an open Thursday Status slot (26 November, "a rule card") to an approved rule with its card.

## The walk

1. Today as staff: the month card, the due list and the Trust line read the same as for the approver. The open Thursday slot was not in the seven-day list (it is four weeks away), so the employee went to the calendar. Calendar, 26 November, the Status slot, "Why this slot" ("Thursday is a rule card: one line, a brand card"), "Create new": the editor opened as a Status rule for WhatsApp Status, planned for Thursday 26 November, 09:00. (2 clicks)
2. The employee wrote the four lines from the slot's own explanation and the Solutions hint in the editor ("The step-by-step job flow, Partner follow-up, the document checklist, the template library and others, in Settings, Solutions"): a question with no owner; the day becomes other people's questions; the question tray gives every question a name and a date; the close as the rule. (typing)
3. "Fill from P.I.S." queued for the runner; the answer arrived as one Status line with the starred phrase: "A question with no owner *waits for you*. Give every question a tray and a name." The live preview showed the yellow phrase on the Status card. The checker reported nothing. (3)
4. Save (4): pc-0003, placed in the slot. "Check with AI" (5): six checklist judgements, "Nothing to change". Mark for review (6): the banner says an approver sees it in Review and on Today and nothing has to be sent by hand.
5. Pictures card, "Make a card in Pictures" (7): the card maker opened on the Status rule template, filled with the line, the starred phrase in yellow. Export PNG (8): saved against the piece. Eight clicks from the slot to a reviewed piece with its picture; the employee never left the path the screen gave them.
6. As Palak: Review, card 24 of 80, the Status preview, Approve (9). The piece is approved with its card; the slot shows it as approved; the employee's next visit to the piece shows the posted-or-approved state.
7. **What the employee could not see.** After "Mark for review" there is no place that lists the employee's own pieces and their state. They would reopen the piece, or ask. Medium.
8. The review card for a Status rule shows a tall 9:16 navy preview with the line near the foot, as a Status looks; on the phone this fills the screen before the actions. Low, by design.

## The content, judged for the employee's output

- **ICP.** "A question with no owner waits for you" is the fifth pain in the brief's list (everything waits for you), in the owner's own frame. The rule names what to do (a tray and a name), which is the brief's "built to be sent" rule: a one-line rule an owner forwards.
- **Storyline.** "The question tray" is a tool we built; the line says what it does rather than naming it, which the brief allows ("or say plainly what it does"). The employee's solution line in the editor names it. Good.
- **Register and channel.** One line, 80 characters, the starred phrase for the card, no offer, no exclamation mark. Fits the Thursday rule card in the plan.
- **What the employee was not told.** Nothing in the editor says a rule card is the close line of a longer idea, or shows the four November rule cards already written, so a new hire could repeat one. A "Similar pieces" line is a follow-up.

## Verdict before the rework

The employee can make a rule card, a carousel or a b-roll script from the slot without knowing the brief, because the slot, the type hint, the Solutions hint, the checker's list and the runner's answers carry the brief for them. The one gap is after "Mark for review": they lose sight of their own work.

## What was fixed

1. Today shows "Your pieces" to every user: the pieces they created or last edited, with their state (draft, in review, changes requested, approved, posted), newest first, so the employee sees the approval land without asking.

## What was left

- "Similar pieces" beside the editor (the rule cards and hooks already in the library on the same idea): a follow-up.
- The Status preview's height on the phone: by design, a Status is a full screen.
